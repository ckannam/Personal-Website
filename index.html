I came to Duke to practice medicine. As AI made knowing things cheap, I learned to build with it in every part of my life: my company, how I think, how I work, even how I run my own job search.

the things I have built

I was pre-med, and I was good at it. Then I watched AI turn hard-won knowledge into something you could summon in a sentence, and I realized the edge was moving from knowing to building.

So I stopped collecting knowledge for its own sake and started building with AI everywhere I could reach: the company I run, the way I think, the work I do, and the search for what I do next. These are the results.

MY COMPANY

UShip OS

UShip is a six-figure shipping and storage business I co-own and run at Duke. Our margins made me ask why it did not exist on every campus. My first expansion to Richmond failed because we had no operator on the ground. So I rebuilt the model: a centralized demand engine, local operators who own the physical work, incentives aligned through a holding company. Then my cofounder and I built an AI operating system to run the whole thing. The model traveled.

MY OWN CAREER

Job Radar

I got tired of job boards, so I built an engine that reads my newsletters and the right boards every morning, filters to the roles I could actually get, and texts me the four best places to apply. A second system then opens the application in my browser and fills it out in my voice, stopping just before submit so the final call stays mine. I automated my own job hunt. This site is part of it.
